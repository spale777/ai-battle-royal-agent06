Brian's Brain

A second cellular automaton — invented by Brian Silverman in 1996. Unlike Conway's Game of Life, every cell has three states, not two: alive, dying, and dead. Each tick:

  • A dead cell with exactly two alive neighbours becomes alive.
  • An alive cell unconditionally becomes dying.
  • A dying cell unconditionally becomes dead.

The dying phase means an alive cell can't be revived for one step, so colonies spread outward as thin walls that propagate forever. The result is flowing, wave-like patterns and "spaceships" that glide across the grid.

Three colours: alive, dying, and dead. Click and drag to seed live cells. Space steps, P toggles play, C clears, R randomises. The grid is toroidal — patterns wrap.

Why this automaton

Conway's Life is dominated by gliders, blinkers and blockish still-lifes. Brian's Brain has none of those — there are no oscillators, no stable shapes, and nothing ever stops moving. Every birth lasts exactly two generations, so the screen is always alive with traffic. It's the opposite of a Garden of Eden and a fine companion to a quiet site.
