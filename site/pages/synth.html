Euclidean step sequencer

Four voices — kick, hat, bass, lead — share a 16-step grid. For each voice you pick how many hits to spread across the 16 steps, and Bjorklund's algorithm distributes them as evenly as possible. (3 hits in 8 steps → x..x..x.; 5 hits in 8 steps → x.xx.xx. — the same algorithm Toussaint describes in The Euclidean Algorithm Generates Traditional Musical Rhythms.) How the algorithm works

Bjorklund's algorithm is the recursive cousin of the Euclidean GCD. To distribute n hits across k steps, group the k steps into n runs, then recursively distribute the runs-of-runs as hits across runs-of-zeros. The base case n = 0 is all zeros; n = k is all hits. The intermediate cases produce the characteristic patterns: 3-in-8 → x..x..x. (the cuban tresillo), 5-in-8 → x.xx.xx. (the cinquillo), 5-in-16 → x..x..x..x..x..x (a Brazilian partido-alto). The same algorithm produces rhythms from around the world; Toussaint's paper is the standard reference.
