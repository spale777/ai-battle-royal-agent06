Flow field

A vector field built from Ken Perlin's improved noise — every pixel of the canvas has an angle, derived from sampling the noise at that pixel's coordinates — and a population of particles that walk along the field one step at a time, leaving a one-pixel line behind them. The result is a kind of painting the noise makes by itself, with the particles serving as the brush tips.

Unlike the strange attractors page, this one is a stochastic field: every visit draws a different image from the same algorithm, because the particles' starting positions are randomised and the noise function re-rolls with the seed. Sharing the URL means sharing a specific image — the seed, palette, particle count, step length, and noise scale are all encoded in the hash, so any combination reproduces itself as a shareable link.

The field is Perlin noise resampled at every pixel; an angle is derived by mapping noise(x, y) (in [-1, 1]) to a full revolution. Particles then walk the field in step-length increments, drawing a 1-pixel line per frame and respawning at a random position when they leave the canvas. The visible canvas is a copy of an offscreen "trail" canvas that fades toward the page background by 0.8% per frame, so old lines decay in roughly 8 seconds and the canvas accumulates paint into a dense layered field rather than rebuilding itself every half-second.

  • ember — burnt orange into cream, the canonical "flow art" look.
  • ocean — deep navy through cyan to white.
  • forest — moss green through sage to ivory.
  • sunset — magenta through coral to gold.
  • mono — graphite through silver to white.
  • paper — sepia into cream, like ink on vellum.
  • violet — indigo through lavender to white.
  • rust — dark brown through terracotta to sand.

The noise is the canonical Ken Perlin improved-noise implementation: a 256-entry permutation table shuffled with a mulberry32 PRNG seeded from floor(seed * 999983), so two URLs with the same seed produce the same field bit-for-bit. The particles themselves are not seeded — their starting jitter uses Math.random() — so two visitors with the same seed see the same field but slightly different paintings; for an exact reproduction, the "copy link" button captures the current frame's URL while the canvas is still actively painting.
